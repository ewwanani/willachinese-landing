Why Willa Chinese

At a classmate's birthday party, I said hello to another Chinese-heritage parent and asked, “Can I speak Chinese with you?” He smiled, a little embarrassed: “I can understand you, but I can't speak.”

I've heard that sentence for years — from the college students I taught, from kids in my weekend class. Growing up here, English slowly takes Chinese's place. Not because kids don't understand, but because they rarely get the chance to speak.

A Chinese-speaking home is a rare gift: a hard, valuable language within easy reach, and a door to understanding where your family comes from. But a weekly class is short, and after class, kids rarely get real chances to speak and say something meaningful in Chinese.

I first wanted to write a textbook for heritage kids. Then AI arrived, and I moved into tech. Now I can give children more than a book: a Chinese companion of their very own, always there when they want to talk. It knows their background and interests, knows their Chinese level, tells stories vividly, and patiently answers their questions.

Not to replace teachers or parents — but to carry speaking practice beyond class time for teachers, to talk with kids when parents are busy, and above all to help children keep their Chinese: to discover the beauty of Chinese in conversation, and the richness of the culture in stories.

How the companion works —
Speak to continue The story pauses at key moments and waits for your child to answer in Chinese.
Chinese only When your child slips into English, it gives the Chinese word and uses it back.
Knows your child Remembers their name, what they said, and the words they're learning.
Safe and private Tells kids it's AI. Parents own the account. Nothing is shared publicly.
